## Abstract (272 words with citations, about 255 without)

In the wake of the generative-AI boom, and amid state calls to roll out "AI+" across society and to keep AI "for good" (zhineng xiangshan), a wave of AI products for autistic children has appeared in China: multimodal early-screening "agents" from state research institutes, home-rehabilitation devices such as the ALSOLIFE home-training Bot, and picture-book generators launched as platform philanthropy. This chapter sets these products against the creative AI use of autistic and other neurodivergent people and their support communities. Combining the concept of sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff and Kim 2015) with critical autism and disability studies of technology (Milton 2012; Yergeau 2018; Keyes 2020; Shew 2023), it offers a critical discourse analysis of public materials from 2024 to 2026 (product pages, e-commerce reviews, press reports, short videos and social-media posts) and fieldnotes from a forum on AI and neurodivergent young people. The products imagine autism as a signal to detect, a deficit to train or a distant "star" to reach, and AI as detector, trainer or "warm" understander; understanding is withdrawn from autistic children and credited to machines and their adult users. Community uses follow a different logic. Instead of receiving purpose-built devices, autistic people, parents and parent-led organisations appropriate general-purpose chatbots and no-code tools: as a mouthpiece for voicing grievances and claiming rights, as a medium for interest-driven creation, and as self-built easy-read converters and assistants. These uses adapt the environment rather than the child, reach beyond early childhood into adult life, and value AI precisely because it "doesn't find you weird": alignment rather than empathy. The chapter thus recasts artificial empathy as a question of who is positioned to understand whom.

**Keywords:** sociotechnical imaginaries; autism; neurodiversity; generative AI; China; disability and technology; appropriation

**3.5 Autism as deficit, AI as trainer: the ALSOLIFE home-training Bot** (AL01–AL03, AL-V2, AL-V5; special educators' counter-view AL-V1, AL-V3)

**3.6 Autism as a distant star, AI as warm understander: 追星星的AI** (ZX01–ZX05, ZX-V1, ZX-V2)

**3.7 What the products share: understanding taken from autistic people**
The three mirror pairs; parents, clinicians and the state as real users; "golden period" childhood; manufactured warmth.

**3.8 Creative AI use in neurodivergent communities** (ND01–ND04)
Six modes: mouthpiece and AAC for claiming rights; repeated speech answered rather than extinguished; interest-driven creation; community-built no-code tools (爱纳's apps, 慧灵's easy-read converter and locally deployed guardianship skill, volunteer-built tools); self-directed scaffolds (ND01); alignment rather than empathy (ND03). Risk literacy produced by the community itself.

**3.9 Two logics compared, and their limits**
General-purpose and appropriated vs purpose-built and delivered; adapting the world vs adapting the child; lifespan vs golden period; autistic people as users and authors vs objects; warmth as relation vs product. Tensions: voices still mediated by parents and teachers; the sycophancy paradox (the same "agreeable" AI is relief for one user and a risk for another); protection vs empowerment; "tokens instead of funding" and the capture of community labour.

**3.10 Conclusion: from empathy to alignment**

## Suggestions on the theoretical frame

**Keep sociotechnical imaginaries, with two adjustments.**
1. *Scale.* Jasanoff's concept is about collectively held, institutionally stabilised visions, usually at national scale. A Taobao listing or a CSR video is closer to a company's "vanguard vision" (Hilgartner's term; **unverified**, check Hilgartner 2015 in *Science and Democracy*, Routledge). The chapter's strength is to show how these vanguard visions plug into state imaginaries ("AI+", 智能向善, 健康中国, 科技助残). Section 3.3 should do that work, otherwise reviewers will say "these are just ads".
2. *Asymmetry.* Imaginaries fit the products well but fit community use less well, because community use is practice, not vision. Do not force it into "counter-imaginary". Treat it as appropriation and making, with its own frame (below), and let the contrast be between an imaginary and a practice.

**Perspectives worth considering**
- **Crip technoscience** (Hamraie, A. and Fritsch, K. (2019) 'Crip Technoscience Manifesto', *Catalyst: Feminism, Theory, Technoscience* 5(1), doi:10.28968/cftt.v5i1.29607; existence checked). Disabled people as knowers and makers who hack and remake technology, with interdependence and access as political goals. This is the closest fit for 爱纳 and 慧灵 building their own tools, and it gives section 3.8 a theory of its own instead of being only a counterexample. My top recommendation.
- **Imagined user and script / de-scription** (Akrich 1992, in Bijker and Law (eds) *Shaping Technology/Building Society*, MIT Press; standard, not re-checked). Lets you read concrete design features (启音's account types, ALSOLIFE's "sit for 5 minutes" threshold, 慧灵's one-letter "y" start) as inscribed users, and community use as rewriting the script.
- **Technoableism** (Shew 2023) and the **"disability dongle"** idea (Liz Jackson and colleagues; **unverified** source, likely a 2022 *Platypus* blog post): well-meant devices designed for disabled people without them. A sharp, readable label for the products.
- **Appropriation from below** (Eglash, R. et al. (eds) (2004) *Appropriating Technology: Vernacular Science and Social Power*, University of Minnesota Press; **unverified**). Classic STS work on users reinterpreting and reinventing technology.
- **Care and its politics** (e.g. Puig de la Bellacasa 2017, *Matters of Care*, University of Minnesota Press; **unverified**). Useful for "warmth" and for care slipping into monitoring (StellarCare, 启音's GPS mode).
- **Free labour / platform capture** (Terranova 2000, 'Free Labor', *Social Text* 18(2); **unverified**). For "tokens instead of funding" and community expertise distilled into free Skills.
- **China-specific disability anthropology** (Kohrman 2005, *Bodies of Difference*, University of California Press; **unverified**) for the state lineage of 残联 and 科技助残 discourse.

Possible combined frame in one sentence: *products as vanguard sociotechnical imaginaries hooked into state AI-for-good discourse, read against crip technoscience practices of appropriation.*
