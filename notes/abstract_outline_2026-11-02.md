## Abstract (≈243 words including in-text citations)

Since the generative-AI boom, a wave of AI products for autistic children has appeared in China: multimodal early-screening "agents" built by state research institutes, home-rehabilitation "robots" sold on e-commerce platforms, and picture-book generators launched as platform philanthropy. This chapter asks how these products imagine autism, and how they imagine AI in relation to it. Using the concept of sociotechnical imaginaries (Jasanoff and Kim 2015), it offers a critical discourse analysis of about thirty public materials from 2024 to 2026 (product pages, e-commerce listings and reviews, press reports, promotional and user-made short videos, and social-media posts), supplemented by fieldnotes from a forum on AI and neurodivergent young people. Three cases are read closely: StellarCare (Chinese Academy of Sciences), ALSOLIFE's home-training robot, and Alibaba's AI picture-book tool. The products imagine autism and AI as mirror images: autism as a signal to detect early, a deficit to train and score, or a distant "star" to reach; AI as detector, tireless trainer, or "warm" understander. Across the cases, the capacity to understand others is denied to autistic people and reassigned to machines, while parents, clinicians and the state figure as the real users. Read through the double empathy problem (Milton 2012), autistic rhetoricity (Yergeau 2018) and critiques of AI's discursive harms (Keyes 2020), neurodivergent people's own uses of general-purpose chatbots describe the relation not as empathy but as "alignment". Artificial empathy in China, the chapter suggests, rests on a prior judgement about who is able to understand.

**Keywords** (draw on terms not in the title): sociotechnical imaginaries; autism; China; double empathy; generative AI; neurodivergence; critical discourse analysis

## Outline

**3.1 Introduction: a wave of AI products for autism**
Opening scene (e.g. ZX-V2, the picture-book tool narrating in the AI's own voice: "我终于学会了和星星世界沟通的方法"). The post-2023 boom; the research question; the argument in one paragraph, loosely tied to the book's theme of artificial empathy.

**3.2 Frame and method**
Sociotechnical imaginaries; imagined users (Akrich, Spiel et al.); critical autism studies (Milton, Yergeau); critical AI and disability (Keyes, Shew). Corpus of 30 items (inventory IDs), selection, critical discourse analysis of text and video, the forum fieldnote; ethics (public posts, pseudonymisation); limits (no interviews, thin first-person material).

**3.3 Context: "children of the stars", parent-led rehabilitation and AI for good**
The 星星的孩子 metaphor; family burden and parent-run rehabilitation; the 2024 seven-ministry autism action plan; platform philanthropy and 科技向善 discourse after the generative-AI boom.

**3.4 Autism as early signal, AI as detector: StellarCare** (QT02–QT05, QT-V1; 启音 QT-V2 as a design echo)
"黄金干预期", diagnosis at 18 months, eye movement and micro-expressions, a "Chinese" multimodal database; care as monitoring.

**3.5 Autism as deficit, AI as trainer: ALSOLIFE** (AL01–AL03, AL-V2, AL-V5)
The child as "a computer with a faulty keyboard and graphics card"; 174 milestones and scores; "3× human efficiency"; one hour of respite for parents. The special educators' counter-imaginary of autism as relational (AL-V1, AL-V3).

**3.6 Autism as a distant star, AI as warm understander: 追星星的AI** (ZX01–ZX05, ZX-V1, ZX-V2)
Theory-of-mind picture books (心智解读), parents and teachers as authors, the child as reader; "warmth" and the AI speaking in the first person.

**3.7 Comparison: redistributing the capacity to understand**
The three mirror pairs side by side; who the products' real users are; the absence of autistic voices and adults; artificial empathy as a chiasmus in which machines gain understanding and autistic children lose it.

**3.8 Neurodivergent people's own AI use** (ND01–ND04)
General-purpose chatbots as mouthpiece (嘴替), thinking partner, and scaffold for getting started; "既不是安慰，也不是共情，而是…思维对齐" (ND03). The forum as a contrast case where autistic people are still spoken for.

**3.9 Conclusion: from empathy to alignment**
What the cases say about artificial empathy; what changes if understanding is treated as mutual.
